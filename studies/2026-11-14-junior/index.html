Junior Gold & Silver Miners: Breadth Near Historic Lows as Weekly Selling Resumes

A −5.49% median weekly decline takes market participation back toward extreme lows. Four decades of daily breadth data add context—but demand careful coverage comparisons.

1. The rebound reverses: selling spreads across 476 companies

Through November 13, 2026, the JUNIOR gold- and silver-mining-related universe fell 5.49% at the weekly median, following a +4.35% median gain the previous week. The average return declined 4.15%. Only 91 stocks advanced, 332 declined and 53 were unchanged—19.12%, 69.75% and 11.13% of the 476-company sample, respectively.

The setback was widespread: 249 stocks lost at least 5%, and 90 fell 10% or more. This is not a selloff explained solely by a handful of weak names. Nevertheless, a sharply negative week does not, by itself, define the next major trend.

2. Weekly MA200 breadth: an exceptionally narrow market

Only 62 of 469 companies with valid moving-average observations closed above their 200-day moving average: 13.22%, versus 18.60% one week earlier. Median distance to MA200 deteriorated from −21.35% to −24.37%. The latest weekly breadth is the second-lowest reading within the 155-week available Weekly Analysis history.

Short-lived rebounds are possible with breadth this depressed, but a durable improvement would require more securities to recover their long-term averages, supported by stronger participation and better volume characteristics.

3. A daily breadth series dating back to 1981: the third-lowest comparable reading

Three separate daily history files record percentage above MA200, count above MA200 and count below MA200, spanning 11,691 daily observations from June 22, 1981 through November 13, 2026. Their length does not imply constant coverage: the number of available securities varied dramatically across decades, including very small samples in the early years.

At the latest daily observation, 62 stocks were above MA200 and 390 below. Thus the daily-history denominator is 452, giving 13.72% above and 86.28% below MA200. In contrast, Weekly Analysis reports 62 of 469, or 13.22%. The numerators match; the denominators do not. Differences in instrument eligibility and data coverage have not been fully reconciled, so we preserve both reported series without treating them as interchangeable.

For a more meaningful historical comparison, we restrict daily observations to sessions with at least 400 eligible instruments. There are 606 such sessions. The three lowest are October 30 (12.42%; 56 of 451), November 3 (12.47%; 55 of 441) and November 13 (13.72%; 62 of 452). The latest reading therefore ranks third-lowest in this coverage-filtered sample—not third-lowest since 1981 without qualification.

The daily recovery to 21.35% on November 9 failed to hold: breadth fell to 13.72% four trading sessions later. This describes an unusually narrow market but does not prove that the low is in or that an imminent reversal is likely. The 400-instrument rule is a pragmatic coverage filter, not a constant-membership adjustment or a predictive threshold.

4. Drawdowns: the weakest median reading in 155 weekly observations

The median 52-week drawdown reached −50.53%, the most negative of the 155 weekly observations in the provided summary history. Among companies with valid drawdown data, 397 (84.65%) were more than 30% below their 52-week highs and 239 (50.96%) were over 50% below their highs.

These measures capture accumulated damage, not merely the latest week's move. Because the tracked universe changes over time, historical extremes refer to the available observation history rather than an immutable fixed basket. Extreme drawdowns can persist for extended periods.

5. Cross-sectional stress: historic MA200 distance thresholds

Approximately 33.05% of eligible stocks fell below their own historical P10 threshold for distance to MA200, the highest share across the 154 calibrated weekly observations in the source series. Roughly 65.67% were below their respective P25 thresholds.

This is evidence of broad, unusually deep relative weakness. Percentile-based comparisons are descriptive and depend on each stock's available history. The results have not been established as statistically reliable market-bottom signals.

6. Volume and relative performance do not yet confirm recovery

Five-day median relative volume was approximately 0.72× its historical reference, compared with 0.80× the previous week. Among high-relative-volume directional moves, decliners outnumbered gainers 32 to 12. The 20-session median signed-volume balance was −19.83, while only 20.38% of the universe showed positive signed-volume balance.

Against the broad-market benchmark used in Weekly Analysis, the JUNIOR median lagged the S&P 500 by roughly 8 percentage points during the week (−5.49% versus +2.51%). The median four-week and three-month relative performance gaps were approximately −11.49 and −18.48 percentage points. Broad-market comparisons are context, not a substitute for comparisons against silver, gold or specialized mining indices.

7. Strong weekly winners are not always longer-term leaders

Despite broad weakness, the ranking shows significant dispersion. TDG Gold (+43.14%), Silver One Resources (+35.14%), Nova Pacific Metals (+33.33%), Radius Gold (+30.43%) and Aurania Resources (+27.78%) led weekly returns. Some of these names nevertheless remain negative year-to-date, while others retain meaningful longer-term gains.

For example, Kingfisher Metals gained +21.65% in the week and stands at approximately +321.43% year-to-date, while TDG Gold and Silver One Resources remained negative YTD. A large one-week move must not be confused with established long-term leadership. 8. Interpretation and conditional scenarios

Observed: Weak long-term breadth, a record-deep weekly median 52-week drawdown in the available sample, extreme cross-sectional MA200 distances and negative signed-volume participation coincide with renewed weekly losses.

Interpretation: The earlier rebound has not yet developed into a broad structural improvement. Although severely depressed participation can accompany turning points, these statistics alone do not validate capitulation or a durable bottom.

Improvement scenario: A persistent rise in MA200 participation, shallower drawdowns and more widespread positive volume would support a recovering market structure. Consolidation scenario: Repeated rebounds fail to broaden participation. Further deterioration: Already-low breadth remains depressed while more companies make deeper corrections. No probabilities are assigned to these scenarios.

9. Conclusion

The latest weekly result represents more than a sharp one-week decline: only 13.22% of Weekly Analysis constituents with eligible data stand above MA200, median 52-week drawdown is −50.53%, and the separately calculated daily breadth is 13.72%. The latter is the third-lowest reading among 606 daily sessions with at least 400 qualifying stocks, even though the daily series begins in 1981.

Price rebounds will matter, but sustained improvement in participation across junior miners will be the more convincing evidence of structural repair. These observations are not forecasts or investment recommendations.
