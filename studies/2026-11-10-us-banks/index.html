US Bank Stocks: Market Breadth Improves as 85% Outperform BKX Over Four Weeks

Published November 10, 2026 · Market data through November 6, 2026 · 180 companies · Benchmark: KBW Nasdaq Bank Index (BKX)

US bank-sector breadth has rebounded, but absolute weekly returns remain subdued. Of 180 tracked bank stocks, 138 (76.67%) closed above their 200-day moving average, compared with 56.11% four weeks earlier. The median weekly return was flat at 0.00% while BKX declined 0.99%. Relative strength is widespread, although that does not establish a broad-based price advance.

1. A balanced week rather than a broad advance

Over the October 30–November 6 measurement window, 87 companies advanced, 87 declined, and six were unchanged. The mean weekly return was +0.05%, while the median was 0.00%. Only one stock rose more than 5%, and none fell more than 5% in the weekly window. BKX lost 0.99%.

Outperformance is not equivalent to a positive absolute return. 142 of 180 banks (78.89%) beat BKX during the week, yet the cross-sectional median was unchanged. This suggests relative resilience and dispersion, not an unambiguous sector rally.

2. MA200 breadth recovers, but has not returned to September's highs

The percentage of banks above their 200-day moving average rose from 56.11% on October 9 to 76.67% on November 6: a gain of 20.56 percentage points over four weeks. That represents 138 of 180 banks above MA200 versus 101 four weeks earlier.

In early September the same breadth measure stood at 95.56%. The rebound therefore remains incomplete relative to that earlier reading. Median distance above MA200 was +3.45%, versus +3.66% one week earlier—positive trend positioning, but without a large cushion.

BKX uses its own index composition and weighting, whereas breadth measures count each eligible bank once. A regional-versus-large-bank rotation is a possibility worth testing, but the aggregate evidence alone does not establish that smaller regional banks caused the divergence.

4. Trading volume improves, without signs of exceptional activity

The median ratio of recent five-session trading volume to the preceding 60-session baseline reached 1.10×, versus 1.01× in the previous reading. Just nine banks (5%) recorded volume at least 1.5 times their baseline: six were up over five sessions and three were down.

The 20-session signed-volume balance shifted more decisively: its median improved from −2.71% to +6.65%. 117 banks (65%) had a positive signed-volume balance, compared with 41.67% previously. This is consistent with better directional participation, but does not identify institutional buyers or establish net investment inflows.

5. Drawdowns: most stocks remain relatively close to their annual highs

The median decline from the highest closing price of the past 52 weeks was −8.15%, against −7.78% the previous week. 60 banks (33.33%) traded more than 10% below their 52-week highs. Fourteen (7.78%) were more than 20% below, and three (1.67%) were more than 30% below.

Median drawdown from the highest close in each stock's full available history was −13.26%. This broader measure can include old price highs and is sensitive to split adjustments and source-history completeness. It is not a measure of the realized loss for any individual investor.

7. Measured evidence, interpretation, and scenarios

What the data show: MA200 breadth has improved, a majority of banks now show positive signed-volume balance, and broad outperformance relative to BKX persists over several horizons. But weekly advances and declines are evenly split and median absolute performance remains flat.

Interpretation: the sector's internal market structure appears stronger than benchmark performance alone would suggest. Leadership may be broadening or rotating; aggregate readings cannot yet identify which bank categories are driving that change.

Constructive scenario: a sustained increase in MA200 participation, a widening positive distance to the moving average, and stronger absolute price gains would add credibility to the recovery. Adverse scenario: breadth contraction, rising deep drawdowns, and deteriorating directional volume would weaken that interpretation.

9. Methodology and limitations

Data inputs are the author-supplied individual history, summary history, weekly workbook, and official publication JSON. The historical sample contains 155 weekly observations from November 24, 2023 through November 6, 2026. The present 180-stock universe is not a survivorship-bias-free reconstruction of the full historical banking industry. Equal-weight breadth percentages differ in construction from BKX index returns.

MA200 uses a simple 200-session average. The 52-week drawdown is calculated from closing-price highs rather than intraday highs. Relative volume compares recent activity with an earlier reference window; signed-volume balance is a descriptive estimate of trading direction, not a measure of net flows. Source data, corporate actions, ticker changes, and price adjustments affect the results. Rankings are separate measures of relative strength and are not personal buy or sell recommendations; proprietary ranking formulas are not disclosed.

Figures refer to November 6, 2026, not real-time market conditions. Read the public methodology and limitations.
