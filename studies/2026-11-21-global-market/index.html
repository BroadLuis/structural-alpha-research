Weekly Global Market Review: From Sector Capitulation to a Broad-Based Liquidity Crisis

Published November 21, 2026 · Data through November 20, 2026 · 17 monitored universes · Structural Alpha Research

This week marks an important change in the nature of the correction. Selling pressure is no longer confined to mining companies, precious metals, or commodities. It has spread to virtually every sector we monitor, including technology, semiconductors, and U.S. banks.

The scanner data reveal an extraordinary situation in terms of the intensity of the declines, the collapse in market breadth, and the increase in selling pressure accompanied by volume.

We are looking at a week with characteristics of widespread capitulation, although we cannot yet confirm that the liquidation process is over.

5. Copper, uranium, and juniors: breadth destruction

The highest-beta sectors continue to suffer the most severe consequences of the correction.

  • COPPER: All 101 companies analyzed declined; 43 lost at least 20%.
  • URANIUM: 76.39% of securities with sufficient history are more than 50% below their 52-week highs.
  • JUNIOR: 200 companies lost 20% or more during the week.
  • JUNIOR COPPER: 86.41% are more than 40% below their 52-week highs.
  • LITHIUM: No company remains above its MA200.

The magnitude of the deterioration recalls liquidation episodes we have witnessed during other major market crises. However, the recovery will depend on subsequent macroeconomic and financial conditions.

6. Technology and banks: the correction spreads

Weakness has now reached the sectors that had shown greater resilience.

This difference suggests that the large companies dominating the index's weighting are beginning to face particularly intense selling pressure.

8. Doctor Copper: the next surprise for the Fed?

On November 19, Tom McClellan published a particularly interesting article:

Doctor Copper Has a Big Surprise For The Fed.

According to his analysis:

  • Copper declined from $6.67 on October 28 to $5.61 on November 19.
  • That represents a decline of approximately 15.9%.
  • Historically, copper price movements have anticipated changes in the inflation rate with a lag of approximately two months.

McClellan suggests that the sharp decline in copper could foreshadow a significant reduction in inflation during the first months of 2027 and warns that additional rate hikes could prove excessive.

This hypothesis is consistent with the recent behavior of the bond market and the extraordinary weakness we are seeing in risk assets.

9. Conclusions: the prelude to the second major bullish phase for precious metals?

The hypothesis I find particularly interesting is that a potential bursting of the artificial intelligence bubble could ultimately trigger a reversal in U.S. monetary policy.

The scenario could unfold in four phases:

Technology sell-off and widespread risk reduction
↓
Broad-based liquidation and pressure on metals
↓
Economic slowdown and lower inflation
↓
Potential Fed policy reversal
↓
New bullish phase for precious metals

In the initial phase, the need to raise liquidity could continue to weigh on even gold, silver, and mining companies.

Subsequently, if the economic slowdown intensifies and inflation begins to ease, the Federal Reserve could be forced to halt rate hikes, reassess monetary policy, and even adopt liquidity-expansion measures if conditions become severe enough.

That eventual policy reversal could become the catalyst for a second major bullish phase in precious metals during 2027.

But we must not confuse the macroeconomic outlook with a technical entry signal. Capitulation is evident in numerous indicators; the definitive exhaustion of selling pressure still needs confirmation.

Over the coming weeks, we will focus on:

  • A recovery in market breadth.
  • A reduction in high-volume selling.
  • The emergence of bullish divergences.
  • A recovery in miners' relative strength.
  • U.S. Treasury yield behavior.
  • Gold's support zone between $3,250 and $3,500.
  • The XAU index target zone between 250 and 260 points.
Paradoxically, the very liquidation process causing so much pain for precious-metals investors could ultimately create the monetary conditions necessary for the next major bullish impulse.

The current deterioration is extraordinary. We should not minimize it, but neither should we extrapolate present conditions indefinitely.

Now it is time to identify when liquidation ends and the recovery begins.
