Global Mining Market Review: Extreme Weakness Diverges from U.S. Equities

A cross-market assessment of mining, metals, agriculture, energy, U.S. and European equities using breadth, returns, drawdowns and volume.

Executive summary. During the week ending October 23, 2026, selling broadened across gold, silver, copper, uranium, lithium and junior mining universes. Meanwhile, energy and U.S. large-cap equities recorded positive median returns and stronger market breadth. The divergence suggests intense, sector-specific mining stress—not a uniform selloff in equities. These are scanner observations, not a prediction of an imminent bottom or a direct measure of investor fund flows.

2. Breadth: mining versus the rest of the market

Only 3.70% of lithium securities and 9.72% of uranium securities remained above their 200-session moving averages. The figure was 11.24% for ASX mining, 12.37% for SIL–SILJ, 13.70% for GDX–GDXJ, 16.81% for junior miners and 21.31% for junior copper. That makes seven—not six—of the twelve universes below 25%. Conversely, energy reached 69.23%, the S&P 500 56.51% and the STOXX 600 54.11%.

3. Drawdowns: structural damage beyond one week

Current 52-week median drawdowns reach −63.30% for lithium, −53.05% for uranium, −47.99% for junior miners and −46.55% for junior copper. Among lithium instruments with valid observations, the share more than 30% below 52-week closing highs is 100%. Equivalent readings include 88.89% for uranium, 80.88% for junior miners, 81.42% for junior copper, 76.04% for SIL–SILJ and 72.41% for GDX–GDXJ. By contrast, energy's median 52-week drawdown is only −9.70% and the S&P 500's is −13.20%.

These figures refer to price declines from each instrument's own 52-week closing high, not from a common sector peak. A weekly return or percentage above MA200 measures a different dimension of market health and should not be substituted for drawdown severity.

4. Volume: directional selling without general volume capitulation

The median 20-session signed volume balance is heavily negative across mining universes: lithium −38.38%, ASX −33.64%, SIL–SILJ −29.76%, junior copper −28.94%, GDX–GDXJ −26.87%, uranium −26.66%, junior miners −26.65% and copper −13.32%. The corresponding figures are positive in the STOXX 600 (+1.69%), energy (+6.64%) and the S&P 500 (+7.08%).

Crucial distinction: none of the twelve universes shows a median relative volume greater than 1.0×. For example, lithium registers 0.93×, GDX–GDXJ 0.77×, ASX 0.74×, copper 0.91× and the S&P 500 0.96×. Directional volume imbalance supports the observation of sustained selling pressure, but a broad high-volume capitulation is not established. Signed volume does not identify institutional buyers or sellers.

5. Gold and silver miners: broad and closely matched weakness

GDX–GDXJ declined a median 9.33% over the week, with 94.56% of instruments down and only 13.70% above MA200. The SIL–SILJ group posted a 9.26% median weekly decline, 95.92% negative participation and 12.37% above MA200. Silver miners showed the deeper median 52-week drawdown (−41.49% versus −36.23%). The similarity in weekly performance does not imply identical company-level exposures or returns. See the complete GDX–GDXJ weekly analysis and its 147-instrument interactive ranking.

6. Junior mining: damage is both recent and cumulative

The junior mining universe includes 482 instruments with a median weekly loss of 8.13%, median 52-week drawdown of 47.99% and MA200 breadth of 16.81%. Among 186 junior copper instruments, the corresponding measures are −7.63%, −46.55% and 21.31%. More than four out of five eligible instruments in each group are over 30% below their 52-week peaks. Junior copper retains modestly stronger MA200 breadth, even though its accumulated drawdown remains almost as severe.

7. Copper: the correction arrived later

Copper's median weekly decline reached 6.73%, with 88.35% of 103 instruments falling. Its MA200 breadth decreased by 18.81 percentage points over four weeks to 38.24%. This breadth is materially stronger than in precious metals or lithium, but the recent participation shift shows that copper miners have not escaped the broader mining downturn. The data are consistent with a delayed correction, not proof that copper must replicate another sector's precise downward path.

8. Energy and U.S. equities: striking counterexamples

Energy delivered a positive median weekly return of 1.11%; only 30.77% of its 169 instruments declined, 69.23% traded above MA200 and the median 52-week drawdown stood at 9.70%. Its signed volume balance was positive at 6.64%.

The S&P 500 universe also gained a median 1.44%, with 56.51% of 500 instruments above MA200 and a positive 7.08% signed volume balance. The STOXX 600 was weaker for the week (median −0.70%), yet retained MA200 breadth of 54.11% and a relatively limited median 52-week drawdown of −13.73%.

These contrasts argue against describing the week's price action as a uniform global equity liquidation. They do not, by themselves, establish direct capital rotation from miners into energy or U.S. equities.

9. What the global anomaly scanner actually signals

The underlying scanner contains 2,860 rows spanning 270 distinct metrics and their historical comparisons, changes and standardized alerts. Its accompanying Top 15 file highlights the fifteen highest-scoring anomalies per universe. Such rankings identify unusual observations relative to each metric's available history; they are not stock picks, probabilistic forecasts or independent votes supporting a directional trade.

Several signals are correlated—for example, counts and percentages of securities suffering large drawdowns. One standardized weekly-change statistic in agribusiness is extraordinarily large in magnitude because the comparison variability is apparently near zero. We intentionally exclude that unstable standardized statistic from economic interpretation. The cross-universe study instead emphasizes directly interpretable observations: returns, breadth, drawdowns and signed/relative volume.

10. Three possible paths from here

Stabilization and recovery

The most damaged mining groups cease making new breadth lows, signed volume balance improves, and the number of securities deepening drawdowns begins to contract. Such developments would support—rather than establish in advance—a possible terminal phase of the correction.

Extended base formation

Price declines moderate but breadth remains depressed; relative leadership becomes increasingly selective while the sector consolidates. An oversold condition can persist without producing an immediate trend reversal.

Further downside

Breadth deteriorates again, signed volume remains sharply negative and more securities reach severe drawdown thresholds. Today's extreme readings alone would not prevent another leg lower.

No one scenario is confirmed by a single weekly cross-section. Subsequent breadth, volume and drawdown observations are essential for assessing a potential turn.

11. Methodology, universe coverage and limitations

Data cutoff: October 23, 2026. Publication: October 24, 2026. Twelve universe-level aggregates are drawn from the Global Weekly Analysis scanner and its contemporaneous anomaly summary. Instrument counts across universes add to 2,555, with constituent overlap. The scanner contains sector-level indicators, not a complete public company-by-company nine-column ranking; publishing an invented all-company ranking would be misleading. The proprietary scanner files, weights, scores, algorithms and private source histories are not published.

MA200 breadth denotes the valid fraction above a 200-session moving average; 52-week drawdowns reference instrument-specific closing highs; relative volume compares recent turnover with each instrument's historical reference; signed volume balance summarizes activity on rising versus falling sessions. Denominators may differ across measures because of missing observations. Comparisons are not adjusted for cross-market currency, risk, survivorship, company overlap or index construction. Scanner anomalies may be statistically unstable when historical variance is small. No statement here is individualized investment advice. Read the public methodology, the GDX–GDXJ companion study and the ASX companion study.

12. Conclusion

The defining feature of the week was not simply falling mining prices; it was the exceptional gap between near-universal weakness in mining-related markets and the comparative resilience of energy and broad U.S. equities. Precious metals, junior miners, lithium, uranium and increasingly copper all exhibit severely damaged breadth and deep drawdowns. Directional volume pressure reinforces the diagnosis, although generalized high-volume capitulation is not confirmed. Conditions justify watching closely for exhaustion and improving internal participation—but they do not establish that a durable mining-sector bottom has formed.
