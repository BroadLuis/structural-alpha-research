Copper Miners: Breadth Deteriorates Despite Resilient Three-Month Relative Strength

The market's three-month relative resilience contrasts with weakening participation, negative volume balance and broad drawdowns. Weekly data through October 9.

Universe: 81 securities, including listed copper-related funds; price observation period October 2–9, 2026. Not a deduplicated company count.

1. Breadth has weakened faster than headline returns

Only 40 of 81 securities (49.4%) remain above their 200-day moving average. On the 80-security comparable sample, breadth fell from 62.5% to 50.0% over four weeks, a decline of 12.5 percentage points. The weekly median return of −0.68% masks a wider retreat: 48 declined, 29 advanced and four were unchanged.

This is not evidence of a uniform collapse, but it is a meaningful deterioration in participation. A sustainable sector-wide recovery would require a broader set of constituents to regain their long-term trend.

2. Volume does not confirm broad accumulation

Median five-session relative volume was 0.77× its historical reference. Just six of 81 securities reached 1.5× relative volume, split evenly between rising and declining names. The median signed 20-session volume balance weakened from −9.3% to −11.3%.

Isolated high-volume advances therefore do not yet establish broad buying participation. Volume remains one of the main obstacles to calling the recent action a confirmed recovery.

3. Drawdowns reveal the underlying damage

The median security is 28.2% below its 52-week high. Among 80 valid observations, 52 (65.0%) are down more than 20% and 35 (43.8%) more than 30%. The accumulated damage is substantially larger than the modest median loss during the latest week.

4. Short-term weakness, three-month resilience

Only 24.7% of the universe outperformed the S&P 500 over the latest week, versus 67.9% over three months. This divergence is the central finding: short-term breadth has deteriorated without fully erasing the relative advantage built over the previous quarter.

The strongest weekly advances were Aldebaran Resources (+30.6%) and Prospect Resources (+27.7%), while Northern Dynasty Minerals (−16.2%) and Gladiator Metals (−11.7%) were among the weakest. These extremes illustrate dispersion, not a broad sector signal.

5. What would change the assessment?

Watch for a sustained rebound in the share above MA200, rising participation on stronger volume, improvement in signed volume balance and a reduction in deep drawdowns. Until these signals broaden, the sector's relative strength at the three-month horizon should be treated as resilience rather than confirmation of a new advance.
