Silver Miners: Historic Breadth Breakdown and Widening Underperformance

Week ending October 23, 2026 · 98 silver-mining and related instruments · Historical comparison across 152 weekly observations since December 1, 2023

1. Executive Summary

The silver-mining sector has entered an exceptionally intense phase of technical deterioration. The week of October 16–23 ended with 94 of 98 securities declining, a median weekly return of −9.26%, and only 12 of the 97 securities with valid data trading above their 200-day moving averages.

Yet the most significant finding is not merely the size of the losses. It is the combination of nearly universal selling pressure, rapidly shrinking bullish participation, and deteriorating relative strength versus silver itself.

One particularly troubling feature is that mining stocks are falling considerably more than physical silver. While the SLV ETF declined 5.42%, the SIL–SILJ universe posted a median loss of 9.26%.

This divergence suggests that the market is penalizing the corporate and equity-market component of silver exposure more severely than the metal itself.

The data are consistent with an advanced phase of liquidation and oversold conditions, but they do not yet establish that final capitulation has occurred or that a market bottom is in place.

The deterioration is not limited to a higher number of declining companies. The magnitude of losses has also intensified considerably.

In the previous week, 57 securities had declined more than 5%. There are now 83, equivalent to 84.7% of the universe. Declines exceeding 10% have risen from 15 to 37 companies.

This describes a broadly distributed liquidation process. The correction is no longer concentrated in a handful of explorers or speculative companies: weakness extends to established producers, developers, royalty companies, and listed mining investment vehicles.

The near disappearance of advancing securities signals a withdrawal of buying participation. However, until that participation recovers on a sustained basis, oversold conditions may deepen further.

3. Long-Term Trend: MA200 Participation Collapses

The deterioration in the long-term trend is even more significant. Four weeks ago, 44.79% of the comparable sample traded above their 200-day moving averages. That proportion has now fallen to 12.50% on the comparable basis. In the current valid sample, the figure is 12.37%.

Percentage of securities above their 200-day moving averages

The reading from four weeks earlier uses a comparable sample of 96 securities; the last two weeks use 97 valid securities. The bars show the three specified observations, not a continuous historical series.

The weekly decline is particularly abrupt: from 26.80% to 12.37%, a loss of 14.43 percentage points in just one week.

The median distance from MA200 has moved from −12.29% to −18.93%. The typical company in the universe is not merely trading below its long-term trend; it is moving rapidly farther away from it.

Long-term breadth confirms that this is no longer an isolated bout of volatility. There is widespread destruction of the sector's technical structure.

Nevertheless, readings this extreme can also precede sharp rebounds. The problem is that an exceptionally oversold market can keep falling before it finds a bottom.

Currently, 73 of the 96 valid companies, or 76.0%, have fallen more than 30% from their annual highs. More than half are at least 40% below their highs, and 36 have lost more than half their value relative to those reference levels.

The 41.49% median drawdown represents a further deterioration of 5.30 percentage points from the previous week.

Moreover, the median decline from the highest price across the full available history is 58.70%, versus 54.54% previously. This latter measure requires caution: companies have price histories of different lengths, and their highs may belong to different market cycles.

Taken together, the data show that this correction is not merely a phenomenon of the last two weeks. Substantial damage is accumulating in price structures that were already weakening.

Signed volume balance measures the predominance of trading on advancing or declining sessions over the last 20 trading sessions.

Its deterioration to −29.76% reflects a pronounced selling imbalance. Only six companies maintain a positive balance.

However, median relative volume has fallen to 0.82 times its usual reference, and only four securities show trading activity greater than 1.5 times that reference. Moreover, none of those four high-volume securities advanced over the five sessions considered.

The interpretation matters: selling pressure dominates, but there is still no generalized explosion in volume that would identify classic capitulation.

What may be taking place is a gradual withdrawal of buyers combined with persistent selling, rather than a final, extraordinarily concentrated liquidation.

The absence of extreme volume does not rule out an approaching bottom. It simply prevents us from claiming that a bottom has been confirmed by a capitulation signal.

This marks a significant change from the October 16 assessment. At that point, silver miners still maintained reasonably balanced short-term relative participation. One week later, that resilience has nearly disappeared.

This behavior is consistent with a phase of reduced exposure to mining equities in which investors prefer the metal to the companies. It does not, by itself, demonstrate that the fundamentals of every business have deteriorated to the same degree.

Long-Term Breadth Reaches Its Historical Low

The 12.37% of companies above MA200 is the lowest reading in the series. Within the available history, there has never been such a small proportion of companies maintaining a bullish long-term trend.

Drawdowns Also Reach an Extreme

The 41.49% median decline from 52-week highs is likewise the most negative observation in the historical sample. The coincidence of these two lows matters: not only are many companies declining, but the accumulated damage to their share prices is exceptionally deep.

Selling Pressure Approaches Historical Extremes

The 95.92% share of declining securities is the fourth-highest in the series. The median weekly return of −9.26% is the sixth-worst observation. The −29.76% signed volume balance is the second-most negative reading.

The combination indicates that deterioration is not solely a price phenomenon: it is also evident in breadth, trend, and the distribution of trading activity.

The Loss of Leadership Versus Silver Is Also Exceptional

Only 17.71% of comparable miners outperformed SLV during the week, the sixth-worst reading in the series.

This reinforces a central conclusion of the analysis: mining equities are under especially intense pressure relative to their reference metal.

Relative weakness can become so extreme that a subsequent recovery follows, but the historical observations examined here do not demonstrate that such a recovery must begin immediately.

The historical perspective does not confirm a bottom. It confirms something different: the sector has reached an exceptionally deteriorated condition within the period examined.

10. Conclusion: Is Capitulation Near, or Are Further Declines Ahead?

The SIL–SILJ sector displays an extraordinarily negative combination of technical signals.

Some 95.9% of securities ended the week lower. The median weekly return was −9.26%. Only 12.4% of companies with valid data remain above MA200, while the median drawdown exceeds 41%.

This is compounded by a sharp deterioration in signed volume and a major loss of leadership relative to physical silver.

The historical perspective underscores the severity of the situation: MA200 participation and the median 52-week drawdown are at their most negative levels across 152 available observations.

These conditions justify considering whether the sector may be approaching a zone of seller exhaustion.

However, there is still a fundamental difference between identifying exceptional oversold conditions and confirming a bottom.

Relative volume does not show a broad expansion, the selling imbalance continues to worsen, and very few companies are able to resist the correction.

Therefore, the available evidence is more consistent with persistent liquidation than with final capitulation already completed.

The possibility of a significant investment opportunity deserves attention, especially if current weakness begins to stabilize. But that hypothesis needs further confirmation.

The first signals to monitor would be a sustained recovery in weekly breadth, a rise in the number of securities above MA200, an improvement in signed volume balance, and, especially, renewed relative leadership by miners versus SLV.

This week's conclusion is that silver miners have reached an extreme technical condition. The decisive question is no longer whether the sector is oversold, but when sufficiently strong buying demand will begin to emerge to halt the deterioration.
