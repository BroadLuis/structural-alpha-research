Equity Breadth Recovers. Mining Stocks Remain Deeply Damaged.

Broad equity benchmarks and energy are strengthening while precious metals, copper and junior miners remain in a severe structural downturn. A weekly bounce is not yet a confirmed recovery.

The defining divergence: equity-market breadth is improving while every one of the eight mining universes in this study remains below 20% participation above the 200-day moving average.

1. A divided market, not a uniform selloff

The October 30 weekly cross-section separates into three distinct groups. Broad U.S. and European equities advanced with relatively healthy participation: 63.6% of S&P 500 constituents and 72.8% of STOXX 600 constituents rose. Energy stands out with the strongest long-term trend breadth, at 70.4% above MA200.

Mining groups tell a different story. Gold and silver miners, copper stocks and juniors posted negative median weekly returns and exceptionally low MA200 participation. Uranium and Australian miners rallied during the week, yet their long-term breadth remains severely impaired. Prices can rebound before market structure repairs itself.

3. The four-week breadth breakdown

The difference is even sharper when examining the past four weeks. S&P 500 breadth improved by 17.4 percentage points, STOXX 600 by 9.1 points, and energy by 13.0 points. In contrast, copper lost 32.0 points, GDX/GDXJ 31.0 points, and SIL/SILJ 29.9 points.

This is too broad a deterioration to dismiss as a handful of weak names. The mining decline is also not merely a reflection of weakness in the major equity benchmarks: their internal trends have been moving in opposite directions.

4. Volume: capitulation or a gradual drying-up of selling?

Directional volume balance remains negative across the mining groups. The 20-session signed-volume balances are approximately −20.6% for GDX/GDXJ, −25.5% for SIL/SILJ, −25.2% for juniors and −31.0% for copper. By contrast, the S&P 500, STOXX 600 and energy have positive readings.

Typical relative-volume readings for several mining universes are below 1.0×: SIL/SILJ around 0.87×, GDX/GDXJ 0.85× and juniors 0.80×. Selling pressure remains dominant, but the cross-sectional figures do not establish a generalized high-volume capitulation climax.

There are two plausible readings. Sellers may be gradually exhausting themselves as trading activity thins. Alternatively, the absence of a decisive volume climax may leave room for a final liquidation phase. Low volume alone cannot confirm a bottom.

Signed volume balance is an indicator of activity on rising versus falling sessions, not an observed net capital-flow measure. Relative volume compares recent trading activity with each instrument's reference period.

5. The depth of the drawdowns

The median distance below 52-week highs is −64.9% for lithium, −55.6% for uranium, −50.0% for junior miners and −46.9% for SIL/SILJ. These are not minor pullbacks; they describe widespread damage across the mining complex.

In lithium, roughly 81.5% of instruments are more than 50% below their 52-week highs; the comparable shares are approximately 52.1% in uranium, 49.9% among juniors and 41.7% in SIL/SILJ. Severe drawdowns warrant attention, but they are not buy signals by themselves.

6. Where to watch for a turn

Silver miners: an extreme structural contraction

In SIL/SILJ, approximately 82.7% of instruments declined during the week, only 6.2% remain above MA200 and the median 52-week drawdown is −46.9%. A potential late-stage correction is a reasonable working hypothesis, but it is not a confirmed reversal. A sustainable recovery would require broader participation and stabilization in longer-term trend breadth.

Gold miners: little evidence of a confirmed recovery

GDX/GDXJ breadth is just 10.3% above MA200 after losing 31.0 percentage points in four weeks. Even if bullion finds technical support, the mining universe has not yet demonstrated a sustained structural turn.

Copper and juniors: weakness remains widespread

Copper's median weekly return was −1.85%, with just 18.8% above MA200. Junior copper miners returned a median −4.44%, while the broader junior universe posted −4.35%. Their long-term breadth remains depressed at 15.9% and 11.9%, respectively.

Uranium and ASX: rebounds without structural repair

Uranium's median weekly return was +1.68% and 63.2% of constituents advanced, yet only 8.2% are above MA200. Australian miners had an even stronger weekly rebound: 72.7% advanced, with a +4.28% median return, but only 19.3% remain above MA200. Both illustrate the difference between a short-term price reaction and a confirmed trend recovery.

Energy: the clear structural exception

Energy combines 70.4% above MA200 with a 13.0-point four-week breadth improvement and positive directional volume balance. It is the strongest long-term breadth profile among the 12 groups studied.

7. What would confirm that the correction is ending?

The most constructive development would be a sustained increase in the share of mining securities recovering their moving averages, fewer companies making new drawdown extremes, and a shift toward more consistently positive signed volume. One strong week, by itself, is insufficient.

One possible path is an initial rebound followed by a retest of recent lows before broader participation improves. Another is an extended sideways base. A further downside leg also remains possible if breadth and volume pressure deteriorate again. These are scenarios, not statistically established forecasts.

The key question is not simply whether prices stop falling. It is whether more companies can participate in, and sustain, the recovery.

8. Methodology and limitations

Data cutoff: October 30, 2026. Publication date: October 30, 2026. Source: Structural Alpha Research Global Weekly Analysis scanner and companion anomaly summary, covering 12 market universes. Each metric uses its own valid denominator where observations are missing. Universes can overlap; aggregated counts should not be interpreted as unique companies.

MA200 breadth measures the proportion of valid instruments above their 200-session moving average. Median 52-week drawdown is measured against instrument-specific highs. Signed volume balance and relative volume are descriptive indicators rather than verified net fund flows. Historical breadth comparisons are affected to a limited extent by routine changes in constituent coverage, including mergers, acquisitions and removals of index instruments; such small changes do not plausibly explain the large 30-point breadth swings seen in several mining universes.

The anomaly scanner ranks unusual readings relative to each metric's available history; its standardized scores are not forecasts or trading recommendations. Conclusion: price has reacted; market structure has not

Equity benchmarks and energy are recovering structural strength, while mining-market breadth remains deeply damaged. The severity of the drawdowns makes a potential late-stage correction worth monitoring closely, but the data do not yet demonstrate that a durable mining-sector bottom is in place.

The next meaningful signal will be a sustained recovery in participation—not merely a rebound in prices.
