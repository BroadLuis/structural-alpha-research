GLOBAL MARKET STRUCTURE: Widespread Weakness, Uneven Resilience

A cross-market view of breadth, drawdowns and volume across 12 equity universes. Latest observations primarily October 9, 2026.

Independent Quantitative Market Research · GLOBAL · 12 universes

Reading guide: Every bar is labeled with its exact percentage or percentage-point change. The charts compare universes independently; they are not a deduplicated global stock count.

Executive summary

Global market internals are deteriorating, but the damage is far from uniform. Across 12 market universes, only ENERGY and STOXX 600 have more than half of their constituents above the 200-day moving average. At the other extreme, URANIUM stands at 16.7% and LITHIUM at 0%.

The key distinction is between relative resilience and confirmed structural strength. Even markets with comparatively shallow drawdowns have lost breadth over the past four weeks. Meanwhile, deeply corrected mining segments face a much longer process of rebuilding participation.

Central finding: the correction is widespread, but its depth, speed and maturity differ substantially across universes.

1. Market breadth: weakness is widespread

The percentage of stocks trading above their 200-day moving average is a useful gauge of participation in longer-term trends. Only two of the 12 universes clear the 50% threshold. ENERGY leads at 61.5%, followed by STOXX 600 at 52.2%. COPPER (49.4%) and S&P 500 (46.9%) are close to that threshold; most mining universes are substantially weaker.

5. Volume does not yet confirm a broad recovery

Relative volume helps distinguish price advances supported by rising participation from moves taking place in relatively quiet trading. Most universes have median relative volume below 1.0×. ASX is near 0.73×; COPPER and GDX–GDXJ are around 0.77×; JUNIOR is around 0.79×. ENERGY and STOXX 600 are closer to normal at approximately 0.91× and 0.96×. URANIUM is the only universe slightly above 1.0×, at roughly 1.02×.

Higher volume is not automatically bullish: it can accompany distribution as well as accumulation. A second test is the share of constituents with a positive 20-session signed-volume balance. None of the 12 universes reaches 50%. ENERGY is around 34%, GDX–GDXJ around 39%, URANIUM just over 21%, and LITHIUM around 19%.

The implication: relative price resilience has not yet been matched by sufficiently broad buying participation to confirm a market-wide recovery.

6. Weekly performance reveals meaningful divergences

ENERGY posted a positive median weekly return of 2.46%, while S&P 500 reached 1.47%. Both delivered positive typical-stock performance despite weaker four-week breadth. By contrast, median weekly returns were −3.26% in LITHIUM, −2.33% in URANIUM and −2.28% in ASX.

These differences reinforce the central message: markets are not moving in lockstep. ENERGY combines the strongest current breadth with one of the shallowest median drawdowns, but its recent breadth deterioration prevents an unqualified bullish reading. STOXX 600 remains relatively resilient; S&P 500's weekly improvement coexists with fewer than half of its stocks above MA200.

7. Three different structural conditions

Relative resilience: ENERGY, STOXX 600 and S&P 500

These universes show comparatively shallow median drawdowns and relatively few constituents more than 30% below annual highs. All three, however, have lost breadth over the past month.

Significant correction: COPPER, GDX–GDXJ, SIL–SILJ, ASX, AGRIBUSINESS, JUNIOR and JUNIOR COPPER

These markets combine varying degrees of breadth deterioration and accumulated losses. AGRIBUSINESS stands out for the speed of its recent breadth contraction; junior mining universes for the depth of their existing damage.

Extreme structural deterioration: URANIUM and LITHIUM

Both exhibit very low MA200 participation and unusually deep median drawdowns. Any recovery should be judged by whether participation broadens, not just by the size of rebounds in a few stocks.

These descriptive groups are not investment recommendations or forecasts of relative returns.

8. What to watch next

A convincing structural recovery would require sustained improvement in MA200 breadth, a declining share of stocks in deep drawdowns, and more widespread positive buying participation. It would also matter whether the improvement emerges across several universes or remains concentrated in a few resilient groups.

Conversely, higher benchmark prices accompanied by weak breadth and negative volume balances would suggest that gains still depend on limited participation. In the hardest-hit segments, early stabilization may first appear as shallower drawdowns and less selling pressure, well before long-term moving averages recover. Those early signs should not be confused with a confirmed trend reversal.

9. Conclusion: participation matters as much as price

This first GLOBAL Structural Alpha study finds broad internal weakness and substantial cross-market dispersion. Breadth declined across all 12 universes over four weeks, but the degree of accumulated damage differs dramatically. ENERGY and the major equity indexes retain comparatively resilient structures, while LITHIUM, URANIUM and junior miners face a much deeper rebuilding process.

Volume participation does not yet support a claim of broad recovery. The central question is therefore no longer simply whether markets can rebound. It is whether rebounds can bring a growing number of stocks back into the advance and turn price improvement into a durable structural recovery.

Methodology and data notes

This cross-sectional study compares 12 predefined universes using the latest market scanner and their weekly summary histories, primarily as of October 9, 2026; LITHIUM data are as of October 8. The four-week breadth changes are based on each universe's common-sample measure. Histories must be sorted by observation date rather than physical row order.

Universes may overlap and differ in size. They should not be interpreted as a single deduplicated global sample. Drawdowns are measured from 52-week highs; relative volume compares the recent five sessions with a 60-session reference; signed-volume balances cover 20 sessions. Figures are rounded. This is independent research, not personalized investment advice.
