Platinum & PGM Stocks: A Broad Weekly Rebound, but Only 5% Remain Above MA200

Published November 9, 2026 · Data through November 6, 2026 · 20 monitored companies · Structural Alpha Research

Our 20-company platinum and platinum-group metals (PGM) universe recorded a 4.97% median weekly rebound, with 13 companies advancing. But only one of 20 companies (5%) trades above its 200-day moving average (MA200), and the median 52-week drawdown remains −52.38%. The bounce is measurable; a broad recovery in long-term market structure is not yet established.

It includes producers, diversified operators, developers and explorers. It is not the complete global platinum industry, a pure platinum-producer index or a capitalization-weighted PGM benchmark. Companies have different exposures to platinum, palladium, rhodium and other metals.

1. The rebound reached most companies, but losses remained uneven

During the week ending November 6, 13 companies advanced (65%), five fell (25%) and two were unchanged (10%). The median return was +4.97%, compared with an average gain of +2.31%. This compares with a median weekly decline of about 4.98% in the preceding week.

Ten names gained at least 5%, and five gained 10% or more. On the downside, four companies lost at least 5%, three fell by 10% or more and one declined 20%. The difference between the mean and median highlights the influence of severe losses in a small sample.

2. Long-term breadth: from zero participation to just one company

MA200 breadth collapsed through September and October. During the weeks ending October 23 and October 30, not one of the 20 companies traded above its 200-day moving average. By November 6 the proportion had recovered to 5%—one company. Four weeks earlier it stood at 10%.

Lifezone Metals (LZM) is the sole name currently above MA200, and its positive distance is modest. This is an initial improvement from zero participation, not evidence of a broad restoration of long-term uptrends. In a 20-company universe, each security represents five percentage points: changes in breadth must therefore be read alongside distance and the underlying distribution of returns.

3. Distance to MA200 is improving, but remains deeply negative

Median percentage distance to MA200 improved from −27.82% on October 30 to −23.20% on November 6, an improvement of approximately 4.62 percentage points. Mean distance remains even lower at −27.62%.

Against thresholds calculated from the available weekly history, 35% of companies fall below the lower P10 distance threshold and 70% below P25; none reaches the upper P75 or P90 thresholds. These are descriptions of an unusually weak cross-section within the available sample, not signals of a market bottom or evidence from several decades of history.

4. Drawdowns remain severe despite the bounce

All 20 monitored companies are more than 20% below their highest closing price of the previous 52 weeks. Eighteen (90%) are more than 30% below, 16 (80%) more than 40% below, and 11 (55%) more than 50% below.

The median 52-week drawdown narrowed from −53.89% to −52.38%. The proportion down more than 50% decreased from 65% to 55% in one week. Measured against each security’s full available price record, the median drawdown is about −65.71%; because the underlying histories vary in length, this must not be presented as a uniform lifetime measure.

5. Signed-volume pressure eases, but buying participation has not become dominant

Median five-session trading volume was 1.04 times the preceding 60-session reference, versus 1.13 times a week earlier. This does not describe a sector-wide surge in trading volume. Only two companies traded at least 1.5 times their reference volume, and both declined; none both rose and reached that threshold.

The 20-session median signed-volume balance nevertheless improved considerably from −34.60% to −8.87%, a change of roughly +25.73 percentage points. Companies with a positive balance increased from four to seven, or from 20% to 35%.

The easing negative balance is consistent with moderating selling pressure, but the median remains below zero and the majority of companies lack a positive balance. Signed volume is a descriptive measure; it does not directly identify institutional buying or accumulation.

6. Weekly leaders are not necessarily long-term leaders

The strongest weekly advances were Generation Mining (+13.58%), Eastern Platinum (+12.50%), Zimplats (+12.00%), Sibanye-Stillwater (+10.59%), Impala Platinum Holdings (+10.01%) and Lifezone Metals (+9.95%).

The first five still recorded negative four-week performance. Lifezone stands out: its four-week return was +23.53% and its three-month return +6.60%. It is also the only monitored company above MA200. A single weekly ranking should not, however, be conflated with the separate global relative-strength ranking.

7. Pockets of pronounced weakness persist

The five negative weekly performers were ValOre Metals (−20.00%), Greenland Mines (−16.16%), New Age Metals (−15.15%), Ivanhoe Mines (−8.41%) and Tharisa (−0.48%).

Over four weeks, ValOre lost 33.33%, New Age Metals 28.21% and Greenland Mines 25.73%. These divergences matter particularly in a limited 20-company universe, where a handful of large moves can strongly influence averages.

8. Capitulation or early stabilization?

The near-zero MA200 breadth, very negative median distance and severe drawdowns document an unusually damaged market structure. Yet low participation and deep corrections do not, by themselves, confirm a capitulation event. The latest median relative-volume reading was not exceptionally high and the two high-volume names still declined.

Observed: a broad weekly rebound, a moderate improvement in MA200 distance, and a strong improvement in signed-volume balance. Interpretation: the evidence is consistent with early stabilization after heavy selling. Unresolved: whether a durable recovery will follow or the move will remain a countertrend rebound. The historical record in this report does not establish reliable probabilities for those paths.

9. Evidence to watch next

A more persuasive recovery would require MA200 participation to expand beyond one company, median distance to MA200 to continue rising, the share of severe drawdowns to decline, and positive signed-volume participation to widen. Conversely, another fall toward zero MA200 breadth accompanied by weaker distances and adverse volume would signal renewed deterioration.

10. Conclusion

The rebound is real, but the underlying sector structure remains weak. Thirteen of 20 companies advanced during the week, while the median stock gained 4.97%. Nevertheless, only one company trades above MA200 and the typical security remains more than 50% below its 52-week closing high. Buying participation has begun to improve but is not yet broad enough to validate a long-term change in trend.

For now, the key question is whether this improvement expands into sustained breadth and volume confirmation—not whether a depressed reading alone must signal a bottom.

Methodology and limitations

Data cut-off: November 6, 2026. The PLATINUM universe includes 20 curated companies, primarily associated with the US, Canadian, South African and Australian markets, with certain UK-traded listings. It includes PGM producers, diversified miners, developers and explorers. This is not a complete global survey or a pure platinum price proxy. Companies differ materially in metals exposure and business risk.

The weekly summary contains 151 observations, from December 22, 2023 through November 6, 2026. The number of eligible constituents changed over time, and security-specific histories have different lengths. Percentiles are specific to this dataset and do not describe multidecade historical records. Cross-sectional measures are not weighted by company capitalization; quoted prices may be in different local currencies. Relative volume compares recent trading with the preceding 60-session reference. Weekly and global rankings represent separate measures, and missing values are preserved.
