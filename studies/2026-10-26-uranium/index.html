Uranium Stocks: Market Breadth Hits a 153-Week Low

Week ending October 23, 2026 · 75 uranium- and nuclear-linked securities · Historical comparison across 153 weekly observations since November 24, 2023

Executive summary

The uranium-linked equity universe ended October 23 under exceptionally broad selling pressure. Of 75 instruments, 67 declined, the median weekly return was −6.03%, and only 7 of 72 valid instruments traded above their 200-day moving averages. The median drawdown from 52-week highs deepened to −53.05%.

Across 153 available weekly observations, both MA200 breadth and median 52-week drawdown reached their weakest readings. This is evidence of exceptional technical deterioration—not proof that a durable market bottom has formed.

Historical perspective: unusually deep and widespread weakness

MA200 breadth is at its lowest level in the available 153-week series; the median 52-week drawdown is also the most negative. The 89.33% share of decliners is the second-highest, while the −6.03% weekly median return is only the twentieth-worst. The distinction matters: current stress is more exceptional in breadth and cumulative losses than in single-week intensity.

Market breadth and trend

67 of 75 securities fell and only eight rose. The share of decliners increased from 56 of 75 the prior week to 67 of 75, even though the weekly median loss eased from −7.05% to −6.03%. This points to a broader, rather than necessarily more violent, sell-off.

Only 9.72% of valid instruments remain above their MA200, down from 11.11% one week earlier and 19.44% four weeks earlier. The median distance below the MA200 is −29.65%. Such technical weakness can precede sharp rebounds, but it is not itself a confirmed buy signal.

Drawdowns and trading volume

The median decline from 52-week highs widened from −49.83% to −53.05%. Among 72 instruments with sufficient data, 64 are more than 30% below their annual highs. The median drawdown from each instrument's highest price in its available history is approximately −84.87%; differing listing histories make this longer-horizon figure less directly comparable.

Median relative volume was 0.78 times its reference level, down from 1.01 times. The median 20-session signed volume balance weakened to −26.66% from −20.11%. Selling pressure is persistent, but the evidence does not show a market-wide volume surge characteristic of some climactic capitulations.

Leading companies and benchmarks

Major uranium-linked names also declined: Cameco (CCJ) −3.58%, Kazatomprom (KAPq.L) −7.61%, NexGen (NXE.TO) −3.55%, Denison Mines (DNN) −4.82%, Paladin Energy (PDN.AX) −6.01%, Uranium Energy (UEC) −7.08%, Energy Fuels (UUUU) −11.22%, and Deep Yellow (DYL.AX) −17.68%. Differences among these companies should not be attributed solely to balance-sheet quality without fundamental analysis.

Reference funds also fell: URNM −3.31%, URA −3.73%, and NLR −4.20%. All three declined less than the universe's median instrument, highlighting the particularly severe weakness in portions of the broader sample. Infini Resources gained 20.00%, while Berkeley Energia fell 6.03% despite remaining up 54.27% over four weeks; isolated relative strength is not a sector-wide recovery.

Interpretation, scenarios and limitations

Measured observation: historical lows in MA200 participation and median 52-week drawdown, alongside unusually broad weekly losses. Interpretation: the market is exhibiting persistent, widespread technical stress and may be approaching an exhaustion phase. Unconfirmed scenario: a sustained recovery would require improving breadth, rising MA200 participation and stronger volume balance; further deterioration remains possible.

This is an equity-market analysis, not a study of physical U₃O₈ spot or long-term contract prices. The universe includes miners, developers, explorers, nuclear technology and utility companies, and ETFs; it is not a pure producer index. Sample composition and data availability vary over time.
