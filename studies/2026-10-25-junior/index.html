Junior Miners: Record-Low Market Breadth Across 482 Securities

The weakest MA200 participation in 152 weekly observations, extreme drawdowns and signed-volume pressure. Full interactive ranking of 482 instruments.

Executive summary. The October 16–23 correction pushed market internals across the 482-instrument JUNIOR universe to extraordinary levels. Participation above the 200-session moving average and median distance from that average both reached their lowest weekly observations since the available series began in December 2023. Signed volume balance ranks second-most negative over 152 weeks. These readings show extreme weakness, not a confirmed market bottom.

1. Weekly performance: the selloff intensifies

Of the 482 ranked instruments, 384 declined (79.67%), 45 advanced (9.34%) and 53 were unchanged (11.00%). The median weekly return fell to −8.13%, compared with −5.15% the previous week. The deterioration was broad and deep: 318 lost at least 5%, up from 253 one week earlier; 164 lost at least 10%, compared with 98; and 17 fell at least 20%, versus eight previously. The median return of the 25 weakest names was −21.33%, down from −17.65%.

Weekly return participation

79.67% declining · 11.00% unchanged · 9.34% advancing. Sharp percentage movements in very low-priced junior securities require particular caution; a large price change need not coincide with heavy turnover.

2. MA200 breadth sets a new low in the available series

Just 80 of 476 instruments with valid data remained above their 200-session moving averages, representing 16.81% market breadth. This is the lowest reading among 152 weekly observations. In a matched 473-instrument comparison, MA200 breadth plunged from 42.28% four weeks earlier to 16.91%, a decline of 25.37 percentage points. The matched-sample figure differs slightly from the current headline reading because the denominators differ.

The median distance from MA200 fell to −21.75%, from −16.54% one week before—also the most negative observation in the available 152-week sample. Fewer names remain above the long-term average, and the typical instrument has moved further below it. Oversold conditions can persist, so neither record constitutes a standalone buying signal.

3. Drawdowns: damage extends well beyond the weekly move

The median drawdown from each instrument's highest closing price over the past 52 weeks deepened to −47.99%, versus −43.89% previously. In the valid 52-week drawdown sample, 93.67% stood over 20% below their respective peaks, 80.88% were more than 30% below, 64.98% were over 40% below and 45.57% were more than 50% below. These thresholds are cumulative and should not be added together.

The median 52-week drawdown is the fourth-most negative in the available weekly series. A separate all-available-history drawdown measure reaches −87.25%; it is not a measure of the 2026 correction alone, because each instrument's full-history high may date from an earlier cycle. The 52-week comparison is therefore the more relevant headline metric for this report.

4. Signed volume: directional pressure intensifies

Median relative volume was 0.75×, down from 0.82× one week earlier, indicating that the typical instrument did not experience an extraordinary increase in turnover relative to its own reference period. Nevertheless, the median 20-session signed volume balance worsened from −17.21% to −26.65%, its second-most negative observation in 152 weeks. Only 75 of 482 instruments (15.56%) had a positive balance, compared with 137 one week earlier.

Among names with relative volume at or above 1.5×, 43 showed negative five-session returns and 14 positive returns. This supports the assessment of persistent directional selling pressure, but does not prove high-volume capitulation or identify which investor groups are selling.

5. Relative performance against the S&P 500

The S&P 500 reference fell approximately 0.47% for the week, compared with the JUNIOR universe's −8.13% median return: a difference of roughly −7.66 percentage points. Only 99 of 482 instruments (20.54%) outperformed the U.S. benchmark. The median relative difference over four weeks was −17.74 percentage points, with just 41 outperformers. At three months, the median difference was −10.11 percentage points, and 153 instruments outperformed the reference.

These cross-market comparisons are descriptive. The universe includes different listing currencies, security types and risk profiles; returns are not volatility- or currency-adjusted.

6. Weekly leaders: rebounds are not always sustained leadership

Only 45 of 482 instruments had positive weekly returns. The ten strongest were Golden Shield Resources (+30.56%, global rank 328), Volcanic Gold Mines (+26.67%, global rank unavailable), Ashley Gold (+25.00%, rank 86), Renforth Resources (+25.00%, rank 42), Sranan Gold (+23.08%, rank 408), Etruscus Resources (+20.00%, global rank unavailable), Terra Balcanica Resources (+16.67%, rank 376), Opawica Explorations (+16.67%, rank 28), Riley Gold (+15.69%, rank 8), and Signature Resources (+14.29%, rank 229).

Weekly performance and broader ranking are different concepts. For example, Sranan Gold gained 23.08% in the week but remained down 72.88% year to date. Golden Shield led weekly returns despite a global rank of 328. The contrast illustrates the risk of interpreting rebound magnitude as enduring relative leadership.

7. Historical extremes: four dimensions tell the same story

Across the available 152 weekly snapshots, current MA200 breadth is the lowest, median distance from MA200 is also the lowest, median signed volume balance is the second-most negative, the 52-week median drawdown is the fourth-most negative, and median weekly return is the sixth-most negative. This combination captures exceptional weakness across participation, price position, drawdown depth and volume direction.

These statistics are correlated, and weekly observation windows can overlap. Historical ranks describe the available sample, not independent statistical tests and not the entire multi-decade history of junior mining markets.

8. Scenarios: bottoming remains a hypothesis

Stabilization and recovery

Breadth begins recovering from depressed levels, signed volume balance improves and fewer instruments suffer large weekly losses. Such evidence could support a retrospective interpretation of exhaustion.

Extended consolidation

Broad selling moderates but most names remain below MA200, while a narrower group begins to separate from weaker peers. The correction becomes less violent without producing an immediate structural recovery.

Further downside

Participation remains near lows, drawdowns deepen and selling pressure persists. Present extreme readings do not rule out another bearish leg. None of these scenarios is confirmed by the latest weekly snapshot alone.

9. Methodology and limitations

Observation period: October 16–23, 2026. Publication date: October 25, 2026. The ranking contains all 482 instruments and preserves the nine fields from the original Weekly Analysis JSON. Some metrics use smaller valid denominators—for example 476 for current MA200 breadth and a matched 473 for the four-week comparison. Price percentages are numerical returns, not volume-weighted portfolio performance. Moving-average participation, signed volume, relative volume and drawdowns are descriptive measures with different observation windows. The full-history drawdown must not be conflated with the 52-week drawdown.

The historical context covers 152 weekly snapshots available since December 2023, not all previous junior mining cycles. Company counts can include different security types and listing currencies; price movements may be unusually large for illiquid, low-priced shares. Global rank is reproduced as supplied, with nulls retained. No proprietary ranking model, internal scripts or private price histories are disclosed. These figures are not individualized investment advice. Read our research methodology and previous JUNIOR weekly study.

10. Conclusion

For the week ending October 23, 2026, the junior mining universe recorded one of its most severe internal deteriorations in the available historical sample. Almost four in five instruments declined; more than a third lost at least 10% in a single week. MA200 breadth and median distance to MA200 reached their weakest weekly readings, while signed volume balance was close to its most negative. The median instrument stood nearly 48% below its 52-week closing high. Extreme readings warrant close monitoring for early stabilization, but they do not establish that the correction has ended.
