Executive summary. A striking divergence emerged in the week ended October 16, 2026. The S&P 500 and STOXX 600 universes each posted a positive median weekly return of 2.68%, while most mining universes suffered heavy losses. The gap goes beyond price: energy retains 65.09% participation above the 200-day moving average, but lithium is down to 3.70%, uranium to 11.11%, and junior miners to 24.37%. Market breadth, drawdowns and directional volume collectively point to a fragmented market rather than a synchronized recovery.

1. The cross-market split is widening

The S&P 500 sample advanced 2.68% at the median, with 71.60% of its securities rising. The STOXX 600 sample also gained 2.68% at the median, with 79.02% rising. Against that strength, median weekly returns were −8.77% in lithium, −7.05% in uranium, −6.73% in copper, −6.36% in junior copper, −5.64% in SIL–SILJ and −5.15% in junior miners.

Energy was the clearest positive exception among the mining-related groups: its median security rose 2.05%, and 62.72% advanced. Agribusiness was slightly positive at the median (+0.43%), although its longer-term participation remained weak. These are universe-level medians, not index or ETF returns.

3. Four-week breadth: damage reaches former leaders

GDX–GDXJ recorded the steepest four-week contraction in MA200 breadth, −34.46 percentage points, followed by SIL–SILJ (−27.08 pp) and agribusiness (−25.71 pp). Junior miners lost 20.81 pp, junior copper 19.89 pp, and copper 18.81 pp. By contrast, the S&P 500 gained 2.81 pp and STOXX 600 gained 5.26 pp.

These four-week measures capture a rapid loss of participation even where three-month relative performance had previously been favorable. They are changes in percentage points, not percentage changes.

5. The median stock confirms the divergence

The median 52-week drawdown reached −60.00% in lithium, −49.83% in uranium, −43.89% in junior miners and −43.01% in junior copper. The corresponding figures were −14.62% for the S&P 500 and −12.45% for STOXX 600. This comparison reinforces that the pressure is concentrated in the more speculative commodity-related segments, rather than being uniform across all equity markets.

Lithium is an especially important outlier: 100% of valid observations are more than 30% below their 52-week highs. That is evidence of deep existing damage, not a prediction that prices must keep falling.

6. Volume: participation is not confirming a mining recovery

The median 20-session signed volume balance is negative across all ten non-broad-equity universes: from −3.29% in energy to −30.33% in lithium. SIL–SILJ stands at −21.26%, ASX at −23.60%, and uranium at −20.11%. By comparison, the S&P 500 and STOXX 600 register positive balances of +1.68% and +4.09%, respectively.

Median five-session relative volume is below 1.0× in most universes, including energy (0.95×), GDX–GDXJ (0.82×), and junior miners (0.82×). Lithium is an exception at 1.24×, but its negative directional balance cautions against treating higher activity as constructive by itself.

8. Interpretation and scenarios

Measured observations: broad equity universes show positive weekly returns and improving MA200 breadth, while most mining groups show negative median returns, contracting breadth and negative signed volume balance.

Interpretation: this is a divergence in market participation, not simply a temporary difference between index prices. Energy is comparatively resilient; lithium, uranium and the junior groups remain structurally impaired. Silver miners and GDX–GDXJ experienced particularly sharp four-week losses in breadth.

What to monitor: a credible mining recovery would require breadth stabilization and expanding participation, a reduction in deep-drawdown prevalence, and improving signed volume across more companies. Continued deterioration would leave the correction unresolved. Relative strength alone does not establish a durable trend reversal.

9. Methodology, comparability and limitations

This study uses the October 16, 2026 cross-universe Weekly Analysis scanner and compares 12 predefined universes: agribusiness, ASX, copper, energy, GDX–GDXJ, junior miners, junior copper, lithium, SIL–SILJ, S&P 500, STOXX 600 and uranium. All scanner observations have the same as-of date. Each universe is summarized separately using its own sample and valid denominators; samples overlap, so counts must not be added into a single global stock total. The STOXX 600 research universe includes 610 tracked securities, not necessarily the 600 index constituents.

MA200 breadth is the share of securities above their 200-day moving average; four-week changes are expressed in percentage points. Drawdowns are measured against 52-week closing highs. Relative volume compares the most recent five sessions with the preceding 60; signed volume balance covers 20 sessions. The comparisons are descriptive, not a proprietary scoring disclosure, an investable index or personalized investment advice.
