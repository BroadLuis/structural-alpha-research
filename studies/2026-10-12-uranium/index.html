Uranium Miners: Selling Pressure Eases, but Structural Damage Remains Severe

Selling pressure has moderated, but breadth, relative strength and drawdowns remain severely impaired. Weekly data through October 9.

75 uranium and nuclear-related securities including ETFs, utilities and suppliers. Data through October 9, 2026.

1. Long-term breadth remains severely impaired

Only 12 of 72 securities with valid data (16.7%) trade above their 200-day moving average. On the 71-security matched sample, breadth declined from 28.2% to 15.5% over four weeks, a 12.7-percentage-point contraction. Weekly performance was broadly negative: 49 declined, 19 advanced and seven were unchanged. The median return of −2.33% improved from −4.82% a week earlier, but still reflects widespread weakness.

2. Drawdowns continue to deepen

The median 52-week drawdown widened from −44.0% to −45.4%. Of 72 valid observations, 57 (79.2%) are more than 30% below their 52-week highs and 34 (47.2%) more than 50% below. Median drawdown over each security's available history is −81.1%, versus −79.7% a week earlier. Available histories differ in length, so this measure is not a uniform lifetime comparison.

3. Heavy-volume selling has eased

Median five-session relative volume rose slightly to 1.02×. The median signed 20-session volume balance improved from −25.2% to −18.9%. Securities declining on relative volume of at least 1.5× fell from 14 to just one. Yet only four securities advanced on similarly elevated volume and just 16 of 75 (21.3%) registered a positive signed volume balance. Less intense selling is not the same as broad accumulation.

4. Relative performance offers little confirmation

Only 15 of 75 (20.0%) outperformed the S&P 500 over the latest week, seven of 75 (9.3%) over four weeks and 18 of 74 (24.3%) over three months. Berkeley Energia rose 44.6% this week, while Piche Resources fell 17.6% and Peninsula Energy lost 16.3%. These extremes reflect dispersion, not broad sector leadership.

5. What would confirm stabilization?

A sustained improvement in MA200 breadth, a reduction in deep drawdowns, more advancing securities on elevated volume and stronger relative performance would make the evidence more constructive. The current reduction in selling pressure does not establish a market bottom.
