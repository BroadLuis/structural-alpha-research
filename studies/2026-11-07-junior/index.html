Junior Gold and Silver Miners: A Strong Rebound from Record-Low Market Breadth

A broad weekly rebound followed the lowest MA200 breadth reading in 154 weeks, but long-term participation remains deeply depressed.

Observed: Gold and silver miners staged a broad weekly rebound immediately after MA200 breadth reached its lowest level in 154 weekly observations.

Interpretation: The rebound deserves attention, but it does not yet establish a durable structural recovery. Most eligible instruments remain below their long-term averages.

1. A strong but uneven weekly rebound

Of 480 tracked instruments, 294 advanced, 133 declined and 53 were unchanged in the week ended November 6, 2026. The median return was +4.35% and the mean was +4.75%. A total of 223 instruments gained at least 5%, while 129 rose by 10% or more.

The S&P 500 fell 2.68% over the same week, and 78.33% of this universe outperformed that benchmark. Over the preceding four weeks, however, only 22.90% outperformed it, using the available observations. The weekly rebound therefore follows considerable relative weakness.

The median weekly gain ranks around the 86th percentile of the available weekly history: a notable rebound, but not an unprecedented one.

2. A rebound from the lowest breadth reading in the available history

The share of eligible instruments trading above their 200-session moving averages fell from 48.95% on September 4 to 11.89% on October 30. The latter was the lowest of 154 weekly readings available since December 2023.

By November 6, MA200 breadth had rebounded to 18.60%, an increase of 6.71 percentage points in one week. Nevertheless, this remains the third-lowest reading in the available history: more than four-fifths of eligible instruments still trade below their MA200.

The key distinction is between a price rebound and a broad-based repair of the market's long-term structure. The former is visible; the latter remains unconfirmed.

3. Historical episodes of similarly depressed breadth

Two earlier episodes in the available history saw breadth fall below 20% before November 2026: February 23, 2024 (19.32%) and July 17, 2026 (19.75%). For each episode, we compare median subsequent instrument returns at four, eight and twelve weeks, using instruments with observations at both endpoints.

Limitation: Two historical episodes are far too few to establish statistical reliability or an independently predictive market-bottom signal. The observations provide context, not a forecast.

Historical perspective: does extreme breadth weakness signal a market bottom?

The recent rebound raises an important question: does an extreme contraction in long-term participation reliably precede a sustained advance?

To explore this, we examined a separate, longer-running historical breadth dataset alongside GDXJ and SILJ price histories. Preliminary comparisons suggest that deeply depressed breadth can precede powerful short-term rebounds, but such episodes have not consistently marked durable market bottoms. Some recoveries were followed by substantial gains; others gave way to renewed selling pressure.

Methodological distinction: The official weekly breadth figures in this article—11.89% on October 30 and 18.60% on November 6—come from our current Python-based research process. The longer historical dataset was generated by a legacy application and reflects a changing number of eligible junior-mining securities over time. The two series are not directly interchangeable, and the historical observations are exploratory rather than a validated trading signal.

Implication: The rebound from exceptionally weak breadth is encouraging, but its sustainability remains unconfirmed. More convincing evidence would include persistent expansion in long-term breadth, narrowing drawdowns and broader positive volume participation.

4. Deep drawdowns remain widespread

The median drawdown from the highest closing price in the past 52 weeks was −48.46%, compared with −50.00% one week earlier. Among 473 instruments with valid data, 436 (92.18%) were more than 20% below their highs, 377 (79.70%) more than 30% below, 311 (65.75%) more than 40% below and 220 (46.51%) more than 50% below.

5. Volume participation: an early improvement, not confirmation

Median five-session relative volume was approximately 0.80× its preceding 60-session reference. Only 57 of 480 instruments (11.88%) reached relative volume of at least 1.5×.

The number of instruments rising over five sessions with elevated relative volume increased from 12 to 29; those falling with elevated relative volume declined from 38 to 26. The share with positive signed-volume balance increased from 15.90% to 21.46%, while the median signed balance improved from −25.35% to −19.08%.

These changes suggest easing selling pressure, but signed-volume balance remains negative on a median basis and broad buying participation has not yet been established. The indicator is a price/volume proxy, not a direct measure of institutional accumulation.

7. What to watch next

Progressive recovery: MA200 breadth continues to expand, drawdowns narrow, and positive volume participation spreads across more companies. These developments would strengthen the case for structural improvement.

Failed rebound: Participation stalls, volume weakens and renewed declines emerge before the long-term breadth indicators recover. In that case, the rebound could remain a temporary move within a damaged market structure.

Neither scenario is assigned a probability. The historical sample is insufficient for a reliable estimate.

8. Conclusion

The JUNIOR gold and silver mining universe recorded a meaningful weekly rebound, with a +4.35% median return and a clear majority of advancing instruments. Its timing is striking: the recovery followed the lowest MA200 breadth reading in the 154-week historical record.

Yet more than 80% of eligible instruments remain below MA200, and almost eight in ten instruments with valid 52-week drawdown data remain more than 30% below their highs. Historical episodes offer useful context but do not establish a reliable bottom signal.

The evidence supports monitoring the rebound closely, while waiting for sustained improvement in breadth, drawdowns and volume before calling it a confirmed structural recovery.

Methodology and limitations

Universe: JUNIOR, a tracked set of 480 gold and silver mining-related instruments, including selected miners, exploration companies, royalties and ETFs. Weekly observations run from December 1, 2023 to November 6, 2026 (154 observations). Eligible sample sizes differ by metric; comparisons use available or matched observations as stated.

Historical forward-return medians are cross-sectional and descriptive. Changes in universe membership, survivorship, liquidity, missing data and transaction costs limit their interpretation. The public ranking is descriptive, not personalized investment advice. See the public methodology guide.
