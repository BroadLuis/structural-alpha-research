ASX Miners: Weekly Gains Broaden, but Only 30% Trade Above MA200

The rebound broadens across Australian-listed miners, but long-term participation and trading-volume confirmation remain limited.

Executive summary. The selected 89-security ASX mining universe showed wider weekly participation over November 6–13, 2026: 54 advanced, 30 declined and five were unchanged. Its equal-weighted median return rose to +1.10% and the mean return was +1.55%. Yet only 27 companies (30.34%) traded above their 200-session moving averages. The current advance is a broader rebound, not yet a confirmed structural recovery.

1. Weekly rebound: gains become more widespread

The proportion of weekly gainers reached 60.67% (54 of 89), and the median weekly return was +1.10%. The distribution is positive on the week, but short-term gains should not be confused with a repaired longer-term trend. The 89-company basket is a selected research sample, not the entire ASX mining market.

The strongest weekly performers were Mindax (MDX, +14.29%), Future Metals (FME, +12.50%), BMC Minerals (BMC, +11.57%) and Argent Minerals (ARD, +11.11%). Their weekly positions are distinct from the separate structural/global ranking.

2. MA200 breadth: recovery from October's low, but still fragile

The share of securities above the 200-day average rose to 30.34% (27 of 89), up from 28.09% in the prior observation. Four weeks earlier it was 32.58%, so the rebound has not yet regained even that reference point.

MA200 breadth sank to approximately 11.2% on October 23. Its subsequent improvement is meaningful, but 62 of the 89 securities still trade below MA200. A rebound can be widespread on a weekly basis while the majority of longer-term trends remain below their moving averages.

3. Volume: selling pressure eases without a broad surge in turnover

Median relative volume declined from 0.90× to 0.73× the reference level. Meanwhile, the number of stocks with a positive 20-session signed-volume balance increased from 13 to 26, and the median signed-volume balance improved from −19.95% to −7.26%.

This is a mixed but somewhat constructive combination: negative participation is moderating, yet only four stocks registered relative volume of at least 1.5× (versus seven previously). Signed volume reflects the direction of price changes weighted by volume; it does not directly identify institutional purchases or sales.

4. Drawdowns still show extensive damage

The median 52-week drawdown was −38.36%. Among 88 stocks with sufficient drawdown data, 71 remained more than 20% below their trailing 52-week highs. This breadth of damage is inconsistent with describing the universe as structurally recovered, regardless of the week's improved advance/decline tally.

5. The weekly leaders are not necessarily the structural leaders

Future Metals (FME) advanced +12.50% this week but remained −68.97% year to date and −47.06% over three months. Its sharp weekly rally illustrates why rebound strength should not be mistaken for sustained leadership.

Benz Mining (BNZ) led the global ranking despite a more modest +0.98% weekly gain, with +137.93% year to date. Solstice Minerals (SLS) ranked second globally, advanced +7.41% this week and retained +434.21% YTD. Other top-ranked names included Alkane Resources (ALK), Ausgold (AUC), Southern Palladium (SPD) and Minerals 260 (MI6). Rank is a descriptive output, not an investment recommendation.

Westgold Resources (WGX) gained +2.33% on the week and +12.62% over four weeks, while remaining −11.47% YTD—a constructive recent rebound within a more mixed broader record.

6. Relative context and the next confirmation test

The equal-weighted ASX basket's +1.55% average weekly return lagged the recorded +2.92% S&P 500 benchmark, with just 30 of the 89 stocks outperforming that comparison. The S&P 500 is a cross-market reference rather than a local Australian mining benchmark, so this should not be interpreted as an industry-matched relative return.

Measured: weekly gainers and MA200 breadth improved, while signed-volume selling pressure moderated; long-term breadth remained low and median relative volume fell. Interpretation: the market may be rebuilding after the October selloff, but the transition remains incomplete. Risks: a renewed drop in MA200 participation, fading rallies on thin turnover or further drawdowns would undermine the recovery case. An improvement in sustained breadth and in participation backed by volume would provide stronger confirmation.

7. Methodology and limitations

This descriptive study uses the current 89-security selected ASX-listed mining universe and the supplied Weekly Analysis records for November 6–13, 2026. The MA200 participation figure uses the available 200-session simple averages. The 52-week drawdown reflects the latest closing price relative to its trailing high, rather than a full historical maximum drawdown. Relative volume and signed-volume metrics are proprietary-data outputs interpreted descriptively. Valid denominators differ where observations are unavailable (88 for the cited drawdown distribution). This is a selected current universe, not a point-in-time survivorship-free reconstruction. Rankings are not trading signals or individualized advice. See the public methodology guide.
