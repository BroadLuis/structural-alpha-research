Gold & Silver Mining Stocks: 95% in the Red Since January 29

Published November 21, 2026 · January 29–November 20, 2026 · 214 comparable instruments

The correction in precious-metals equities has entered an exceptionally severe phase. Of the 214 comparable instruments in our gold and silver mining universe, 204 (95.3%) are now trading below their starting prices since January 29. The equal-weighted average return is −41.0% and the median is −43.5%. Only 10 instruments remain positive.

1. The scale of the correction

Measured from each instrument's highest closing price within the observation window, 195 securities (91.1%) are at least 30% below their highs, and 111 (51.9%) are at least 50% below them. The average maximum peak-to-trough drawdown is −53.6%, compared with the average current drawdown of −51.3%. The median instrument is trading 50.4% below its period high.

Measured from January 29 instead of the individual highs, 155 instruments (72.4%) have lost at least 30% and 77 (36.0%) at least 50%. These are different measurements, but both point to broad-based damage.

The dispersion across major ETFs also illustrates the damage: GDX has lost 36.65%, GDXJ 43.44%, SIL 45.52% and SILJ 50.51% since January 29. The silver proxy SLV has declined 64.98%. In contrast, the strongest-ranked instrument, Panoro Minerals, remains 60.00% above its initial price, despite a 59.39% drawdown from its period high. Relative resilience does not necessarily mean absolute strength.

The selling is no longer confined to junior and speculative names. For a durable recovery, I would look for easing selling pressure, improving breadth and evidence that the larger index constituents have stabilized. The severity of the correction is compatible with capitulation, but it is not confirmation of a bottom. A long-term precious-metals bull-market thesis remains possible, even if additional liquidity-driven selling precedes a recovery.

4. Methodology and limitations

The original source universe contains 215 instruments; 214 have comparable coverage from January 29, 2026 (one starts February 2). SIND was excluded for insufficient coverage. The ranked list mixes gold and silver mining companies, developers, royalty companies and selected mining-related ETFs or precious-metals proxies, including GLD and SLV. It is therefore not a pure 214-miner index.

Period returns represent change in the quoted closing price, excluding distributions, trading costs and currency normalization. Each security is treated equally when calculating cross-sectional averages; the results do not replicate a capitalization-weighted index. The observation window ends November 20, 2026; 212 instruments have that closing date, one ends November 19 and one November 17.

Current drawdown = final close relative to the highest close within the measurement period. Maximum drawdown = greatest observed peak-to-trough percentage decline in that period. These are different statistics. All aggregate results relate to this defined universe and window, not to the full precious-metals industry.
