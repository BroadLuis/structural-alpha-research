Gold & Silver Mining Stocks: 87% in the Red Since January 29

Published November 13, 2026 · January 29–November 13, 2026 · 214 comparable instruments

A broad and punishing correction has swept across precious-metals equities. In our sample of 214 comparable gold- and silver-mining-linked instruments, 187 (87.4%) have negative returns since January 29. The equal-weighted average return is −27.9% and the median is −30.3%. Only 27 instruments show gains.

1. The scale of the correction

Measured from each instrument's highest closing price within the observation window, 149 securities (69.6%) are at least 30% below their highs. The average maximum peak-to-trough drawdown within the window is −48.9%, a separate and more severe measure than the −40.7% average current drawdown.

2. What these numbers do — and do not — tell us

Such widespread declines are evidence of substantial damage and a difficult psychological environment. They do not, on their own, prove that a durable bottom has formed. Deep corrections can persist, and price recovery requires confirmation.

Nevertheless, a painful decline in mining shares is not necessarily incompatible with a broader precious-metals bull-market thesis. The key distinction is between the long-term hypothesis and what market breadth and price structure are confirming today. Patience is important; so is disciplined risk management.

4. Methodology and limitations

The original source universe contains 215 instruments; 214 have a comparable observation from January 29, 2026. The remaining instrument was excluded for insufficient coverage. The ranked list mixes gold and silver mining companies, developers, royalty companies and selected mining-related ETFs or precious-metals proxies, including GLD and SLV. It is therefore not a pure 214-miner index.

Period returns represent change in the quoted closing price, excluding distributions, trading costs and currency normalization. Each security is treated equally when calculating cross-sectional averages; the results do not replicate a capitalization-weighted index. Calendar differences mean the final observation may differ slightly among exchanges.

Current drawdown = final close relative to the highest close within the measurement period. Maximum drawdown = greatest observed peak-to-trough percentage decline in that period. These are different statistics. All aggregate results relate to this defined universe and window, not to the full precious-metals industry.
