Junior Mining Stocks: A Powerful Rally Meets a Severe Breadth Breakdown

A historical study of 452 junior miners since 29 February 2024: exceptional cumulative returns, deep individual drawdowns, and an extraordinarily broad correction.

Executive summary. Of 452 junior mining companies, 334 (73.9%) still trade above their starting prices in this study, despite a median current drawdown of 51.31% from individual highs. Long-term breadth fell to 17.56% above the 200-session moving average on October 23 before a marginal rebound to 17.75% on October 26. The data reveal severe technical damage, but not a confirmed market bottom.

1. A rally that remains visible beneath the correction

From February 29, 2024 through the latest available observations on October 26, 2026, the median cumulative return across the 452-company ranking was +127.27%. A total of 334 companies (73.9%) posted positive cumulative returns, 112 were negative, and six were unchanged. This is an unusually powerful long-cycle performance profile, even after the recent selloff.

These are cross-sectional company returns, not the realized performance of a continuously rebalanced portfolio. The starting and final observations differ among securities. In particular, not every company's last available closing price is dated October 26; the ranking preserves each security's latest valid observation.

2. The drawdown paradox

The median current drawdown is −51.31% and the median maximum drawdown during the study is −61.49%. 240 of the 452 companies are currently at least 50% below their individual period highs. A stock can still show a triple-digit gain since February 2024 while having surrendered more than half its peak market value.

The distinction matters: cumulative return measures change from the first valid study observation; current drawdown measures the decline from the highest close reached during the measured interval. Neither is a substitute for the other.

3. Breadth collapsed during September and October

Across the audited 452-security universe, the share of eligible stocks above their 200-session moving average fell from 50.23% on August 28 to 17.56% on October 23, recovering only to 17.75% on October 26. Participation above the 50-session average dropped from 78.14% to 6.83% over the same period, before a modest recovery to 7.67%.

These percentages use the subset of securities with valid closes and sufficient historical observations at each measurement date. They should not be interpreted as a fixed daily denominator of 452.

4. New lows and the first signs of stabilization

On October 23, 40.00% of eligible securities registered a new 20-session closing low, while 9.51% registered a new 252-session closing low. By October 26, those shares had eased to 26.38% and 6.95%, respectively.

That one-session improvement is encouraging, but it does not establish a bullish breadth divergence. A confirmed divergence would require a sustained contraction in new lows while price weakness persists, followed by broader recovery in trend participation. Oversold conditions alone cannot establish a durable bottom.

5. Two plausible paths from here

Recovery scenario

A sustained fall in new lows, followed by rising MA50 and MA200 participation, would indicate that selling pressure is losing breadth and that more individual trends are being rebuilt. Relative-strength leadership among the least damaged stocks would be an additional constructive signal.

Prolonged deterioration scenario

If recoveries fail to restore participation and the proportion of 252-session lows rises again, the correction could continue eroding cumulative gains. The historical strength of the cycle does not immunize the universe against further declines.

7. Methodology and limitations

The ranking comprises 452 reconciled securities from a collection of 488 adjusted daily OHLCV histories. Ticker suffix differences were reconciled, and individual cumulative returns and drawdowns were cross-checked against the source workbook. No additional split or corporate-action adjustments were applied.

The breadth history uses the same 452-security reference universe but requires a valid close and sufficient lookback for each measure. Moving averages use 50 and 200 closes; new lows are based on 20 and 252 previous closes. The denominator therefore varies across observations. This is a present-day selected universe, not a fully survivorship-bias-free historical reconstruction; delisted or otherwise absent historical constituents are not represented. Cross-sectional medians are not investable index returns.

For additional definitions and caveats, see the public methodology guide. Compare this long-cycle analysis with the recent junior-miner weekly breadth study, which covers a different universe and should not be combined numerically without adjustment.

Conclusion

The junior mining universe presents a striking contradiction: a powerful multi-year advance and an extraordinarily deep current correction. The majority of stocks retain gains from their study starting points, but trend participation has collapsed and individual drawdowns remain severe. The October 26 stabilization is a first observation, not proof of a bottom. A durable recovery must show up in the breadth of participation, not merely in a brief rebound in prices.
